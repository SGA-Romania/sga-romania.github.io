International School on Silicate Melts

From atomic structure to geological samples, to outcrops and large-scale magma chambers

Date: 26–29 April 2027 Location: Faculty of Geology and Geophysics, University of Bucharest

This International School on Silicate Melts provides a research-oriented introduction to the simulation and analysis of magmas, lavas, melts and fluids. It is intended for students and researchers in geology, geophysics, geochemistry, mineral physics and related fields who want to connect the macroscopic behaviour of geological materials with the processes taking place at the atomic scale.

The lectures examine silicate magmas and lavas across a wide range of spatial scales, from atomic structure and chemical bonding to transport processes, mineralizing systems and continental-scale geological phenomena. The school introduces ab initio calculations and molecular dynamics, then shows how atomistic analysis can be used to determine structural, thermodynamic and transport properties. These results are related to broader geological questions concerning magma behaviour, volcanic processes, mineral resources and the movement of matter through planetary materials.

Scientific scope

The programme is structured around the idea that magma behaviour can be understood only by connecting atomic-scale interactions to macro-scale geological processes. Participants will explore how the composition, structure and dynamics of silicate melts determine properties such as diffusion, viscosity, thermal behaviour and how these controls shape volcanic systems and mineralizing environments.

Practical work

The hands-on tutorials focus on the atomistic description of melts and fluids. Participants will use VMD to visualize molecular-dynamics trajectories and to examine how atomic systems change with time. Practical exercises with the UMD/MAGMATOMIX software will then show how to extract and interpret structural information, thermodynamic quantities and transport properties, including diffusion and viscosity.

Programme details

  • Research-oriented scientific lectures
  • Atomistic and molecular dynamics-based methods
  • Visualization of melt and fluid structures
  • Thermodynamic and transport-property analysis
  • Connections between atomic-scale behaviour and geological systems
